## Key Takeaways — Hospitalisation Predictive Models in CKD for Dialysis Patients

* Artificial Intelligence enables a shift in nephrology from **reactive care to proactive, predictive care**, allowing clinicians to anticipate adverse outcomes before they occur.

* Machine learning models outperform traditional statistical methods in handling **complex, multi-variable clinical data** and generating personalized risk predictions.

* AI applications in nephrology already span multiple domains, including **AKI prediction, CKD progression, dialysis optimization, transplant outcomes, imaging analysis, and renal pathology**.

* Mortality risk in hemodialysis patients is high, and conventional models like Cox regression are **insufficient for individualized decision-making**.

* Machine learning models, particularly **XGBoost**, demonstrated superior predictive performance for **1-year, 4-year, and 7-year mortality** in dialysis patients.

* Key predictors of mortality include **age, ischemic heart disease, ejection fraction, serum albumin levels, and neutrophil count**, highlighting the importance of both cardiac and inflammatory status.

* External validation is essential to confirm **model robustness and generalizability** across different clinical settings.

* Translating AI models into **clinical decision-support tools** allows real-time use at the bedside, supporting decisions such as transplant eligibility.

* AI can transform routine clinical inputs into **actionable risk scores**, enabling more confident and evidence-based clinical decisions.

* Fluid management in dialysis is complex and dynamic, and AI offers a way to **personalize dry weight estimation** using integrated multimodal data.

* AI models can predict **intradialytic hypotension (IDH)** well before onset, allowing preventive interventions that reduce complications and hospitalizations.

* Hospitalization risk in CKD and dialysis patients is high and driven by **volume overload, infections, and cardiovascular events**, which are poorly captured by static risk scores.

* AI-based hospitalization prediction models can forecast **short-term (30-day and 90-day) unplanned admissions**, enabling early preventive strategies.

* Potential predictors of hospitalization include **interdialytic weight gain, low albumin, frequent IDH episodes, and elevated CRP**, reinforcing the role of inflammation and fluid balance.

* Integrating EMRs, dialysis machine data, and laboratory results is critical for building **holistic and clinically meaningful AI models**.

* AI-driven nephrology emphasizes **clinical decision support**, not replacement of clinicians, ensuring human oversight remains central.

* Overall, AI has the potential to **reduce morbidity, lower healthcare costs, and improve long-term outcomes** for CKD and dialysis patients through personalized, data-driven care.
